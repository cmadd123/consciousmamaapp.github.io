What should a 2-year-old eat? Meal ideas for a typical day

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Three meals and one or two snacks a day, mostly the same food the family eats[1]. A typical day might be an egg, toast, and fruit for breakfast; half a sandwich with soft vegetables for lunch; and 2 ounces of fish, meat, or beans with a third of a cup each of grains and vegetables for dinner[2].

How a 2-year-old’s eating changes

By age 2, toddlers can eat the same food as the rest of the family, and their diet should include three meals plus one or two snacks a day[1]. It’s also normal for toddlers to choose from a limited number of foods[1]. The AAP’s advice is to avoid fixating on amounts, offer a choice of two or three healthy options, and keep introducing new foods[1].

A sample day for a 2-year-old

Adapted from the AAP’s sample menu for a 2-year-old weighing about 27 pounds[2]:

Sample menu (about 27 lb child)
MealWhat to offer
Breakfast1 egg; ½ cup iron-fortified, low-sugar cereal or 1 slice whole-grain toast; ⅓ cup fruit; ½ cup low-fat or nonfat milk[2]
Morning snack4 crackers with cheese or hummus, or ½ cup cut-up fruit; water[2]
Lunch½ sandwich with 1 oz meat or fish, cheese, and a vegetable; 2–3 soft-cooked carrot sticks; ½ cup berries; ½ cup milk[2]
Afternoon snack½ cup yogurt with 1 tbsp nut butter; ½ thinly sliced apple or ⅓ cup cut-up grapes[2]
Dinner2 oz fish, meat, or cooked beans; ⅓ cup whole-grain pasta, rice, or potato; ⅓ cup vegetables; ½ cup milk or yogurt[2]

Meal ideas to rotate

<em>Breakfast:</em>

  • Scrambled egg with toast fingers and berries
  • Oatmeal with mashed banana and a swirl of nut butter
  • Whole-grain waffle with yogurt and diced fruit
  • Mini bean-and-cheese breakfast burrito

<em>Lunch:</em>

  • Hummus, pita triangles, cucumber, and cheese cubes
  • Leftover pasta with peas
  • Quesadilla wedges with avocado
  • Tuna or egg salad half sandwich with soft fruit

<em>Dinner (from the family meal):</em>

  • Meatballs, pasta, and steamed broccoli
  • Salmon, rice, and green beans
  • Chicken tacos, deconstructed
  • Lentil soup with bread for dipping
  • Sheet-pan chicken with soft potatoes and carrots

Drinks and safety at age 2

  • Milk: from age 2, the AAP suggests moving to low-fat or nonfat milk, about 16 ounces a day[1]; its drinks guidance gives 16–24 ounces for ages 2 to 5[3].
  • Water: 8–40 ounces (1–5 cups) a day for ages 2 to 5[3].
  • Juice: if offered, no more than 4 ounces a day at ages 2 to 3; whole fruit is preferred[3].
  • Choking: hot dogs (unless quartered lengthwise and sliced), nuts, whole grapes, popcorn, round hard candies, and chunks of peanut butter can block the windpipe[1].
  • Keep cutting food into pieces no larger than half an inch and supervise meals[4].

Snack ideas for a 2-year-old

  • Yogurt with diced fruit
  • Cheese and whole-grain crackers
  • Hummus with soft pita
  • Half a banana with a thin spread of nut butter
  • A hard-boiled egg with cucumber
  • Mini muffin and berries

Plan snacks as small meals with a mix of food groups, and serve them at the table rather than on the go.

What to do

  1. Plan family meals and serve your 2-year-old a small portion of each part.
  2. Include one food your child usually accepts at every meal.
  3. Offer two or three healthy choices rather than asking an open-ended "what do you want?"[1]
  4. Serve new foods in small amounts and wait a week or two before trying again[5].
  5. Never force your child to eat something they don’t want[1].

Frequently asked questions

How much should a 2-year-old eat?

Less than parents expect. The AAP sample menu uses portions like 2 ounces of protein and ⅓ cup each of grains and vegetables at dinner[2]. Try not to fixate on amounts[1].

Is it normal for a 2-year-old to eat only a few foods?

Yes. Toddlers commonly choose from a limited number of foods. Keep offering variety without pressure[1].

Should my 2-year-old still drink whole milk?

Most children can move to low-fat or nonfat milk at age 2[1][3]. Check with your pediatrician if your child has growth concerns.

How many snacks should a 2-year-old have?

One or two a day, alongside three meals[1]. Schedule them so your child still comes to meals hungry.

What finger foods are good for a 2-year-old?

Soft cooked vegetable pieces, cheese cubes, quartered grapes, toast fingers, pasta shapes, and small pieces of chicken all work. Cut round foods and keep pieces small[4].
