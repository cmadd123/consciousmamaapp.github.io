When can babies have honey?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Not until after their first birthday. Honey can carry botulism spores, which can grow in a baby’s intestines and cause infant botulism, a serious illness[1]. Don’t add honey to your baby’s food, water, formula, or pacifier before 12 months[2]. From age 1, honey is considered safe, though it’s still a sweetener best kept small[1][2].

Why honey isn’t safe for babies

Honey is a known source of botulism spores. In a baby’s immature gut, the spores can germinate and produce toxin, causing infant botulism[1]. The AAP recommends not giving honey to babies younger than 12 months[1], and CDC and the NHS say the same[2][3].

The age rule applies to honey itself. None of the sources we reviewed make exceptions for raw, pasteurized, organic, or specialty honeys.

Where honey shows up

Plain honey is easy to avoid. It’s easier to miss when it’s an ingredient. Before 12 months, check labels on:

  • Honey-sweetened yogurts and baby snacks
  • Breakfast cereals and granola
  • Honey graham crackers and teething biscuits
  • Homemade baked goods, dressings, and marinades
  • Natural cough or cold remedies

The sources we checked don’t address baked goods that contain honey. If you’re unsure about a specific food, ask your pediatrician.

Signs of infant botulism

Infant botulism is rare, but it’s worth knowing the early signs. They can appear 3 to 30 days after exposure to the spores[1]:

  • Constipation[1]
  • Trouble feeding, sucking, or swallowing[1]
  • Poor head control and drooping eyelids[1]
  • A weak or different-sounding cry[1]
  • Less facial expression and general weakness or floppiness[1]

Call your pediatrician right away if you notice these signs, especially if your baby may have eaten honey.

After the first birthday

Honey is considered safe for children 1 year and older[1]. It’s still a sweetener, though, and CDC advises that children younger than 24 months not have added sugars[2]. Use it sparingly. Breastfeeding parents can eat honey[1].

Other foods to avoid before 12 months

Honey is one of a short list of foods to keep off the menu in the first year. Others include cow’s milk as a main drink, juice, and unpasteurized milk, yogurt, cheese, and juice, which can carry harmful bacteria[2]. Whole nuts are a choking risk well beyond the first year[3]. Plan the first year of meals around simple, single foods and adapted family dishes without these.

Other ways to add sweetness before age 1

Babies don’t need sweeteners, but if you want to make cereal, yogurt, or baked goods more appealing, fruit works well:

Instead of honey in…Try…
Oatmeal or cerealMashed banana, grated apple, or puréed pear
Plain yogurtMashed berries or puréed mango
Homemade muffins or pancakesMashed banana or unsweetened applesauce
SmoothiesRipe banana or frozen fruit

Remember that from 12 months, honey is safe from a botulism point of view[1], but it is still a sweetener. Keep added sweeteners minimal for toddlers[2].

More quick answers

  • Can I put honey on my baby’s pacifier? No. CDC specifically warns against adding honey to a baby’s pacifier before 12 months[2].

What to do

  1. Keep honey out of your baby’s food and drinks until after 12 months.
  2. Read labels on cereals, yogurts, and snacks for honey as an ingredient.
  3. Sweeten baby food with fruit instead: mashed banana, puréed pear, or cooked apple.
  4. Tell grandparents and caregivers about the rule, especially for pacifiers and cough remedies.
  5. If your baby has had honey, watch for the signs above and call your pediatrician with any concerns.

Frequently asked questions

Why can babies have honey at 1 but not before?

In a young baby’s gut, botulism spores from honey can grow and produce toxin[1]. The AAP uses 12 months as the cutoff and considers honey safe from age 1[1].

My baby accidentally ate honey. What should I do?

Don’t panic — infant botulism is rare. Watch for signs like constipation, poor feeding, weak cry, or floppiness over the following weeks, and call your pediatrician to talk it through[1].

Can I eat honey while breastfeeding?

Yes. Breastfeeding parents may eat honey[1].

Is honey good for a baby’s cough?

Not under 12 months. Don’t give honey in any form before the first birthday[2]. Ask your pediatrician about safe ways to ease a baby’s cough.

Is corn syrup safe for babies?

The AAP botulism page we reviewed doesn’t address corn syrup. Babies don’t need any added sweeteners, so the simplest choice is to avoid them and ask your pediatrician about specific products.
