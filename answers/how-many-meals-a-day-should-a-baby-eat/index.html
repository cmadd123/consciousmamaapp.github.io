Starting solids

How many meals a day should a baby eat?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

It builds gradually. Around 6 months, start with small amounts — babies don’t need three meals a day at first[1]. WHO suggests 2–3 meals a day at 6–8 months and 3–4 from 9 months[2]. By about 10 months, most babies eat three meals a day[1], while breast milk or formula stays the main source of nutrition until 12 months[3].

How much at each meal

Start with 1 or 2 tablespoons of food[3]. Once your baby is eating daily meals, the AAP suggests a typical meal is about 4 ounces — the amount in one small jar of strained baby food[4]. Amounts vary from day to day; CDC notes that over a week, your baby should get the foods and nutrients they need[3].

Fitting meals around milk and naps

Early on, many parents offer solids about an hour after a milk feed, when the baby is awake and interested but not ravenous. Avoid starting a new food right before a nap or when your baby is overtired. As solids increase, meals naturally settle into breakfast, lunch, and dinner, and milk feeds happen around them.

Signs your baby is ready for more meals

  • Finishes meals eagerly and leans in for more.
  • Shows interest when others are eating.
  • Handles thicker textures and is starting to pick up food.
  • Seems hungry between milk feeds once solids are established.

If your baby is content with fewer meals, there’s no need to rush — follow their cues[2].

Follow your baby’s cues

WHO recommends responsive feeding: recognizing and responding to signs of hunger and fullness[2]. A baby who leans in and opens their mouth is ready for more. A baby who turns away, closes their mouth, or pushes the spoon away is done.

  • Don’t force food. If your baby refuses, wait and try again another time[4].
  • Offer finger foods as part of each meal from around 7 to 9 months, which helps babies learn to feed themselves[1].
  • Sit down together for family meals as much as possible so your baby can copy you[1].

What to do

  1. Start with one small meal a day at a time your baby is alert and calm.
  2. Add a second meal once your baby is eating happily, then a third.
  3. Keep offering breast milk or formula as usual throughout the first year.
  4. Include iron-rich foods like meat, beans, lentils, or iron-fortified cereal regularly.
  5. Ask your pediatrician if your baby seems uninterested in solids for a long stretch or isn’t gaining weight.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a day should a 6-month-old eat solids?

Start small — many babies begin with one small meal and build up. WHO suggests 2–3 meals a day at 6–8 months[2], and babies don’t need three meals right away[1].

Should solids replace milk feeds?

No. Breast milk or formula remains the main source of nutrition from 6 to 12 months; solids gradually make up more of the diet[3].

When do babies start snacks?

Around the first birthday, most toddlers move to about three meals and two or three snacks a day[3].

My baby eats a lot one day and little the next. Is that normal?

Yes. Intake varies day to day; look at the week as a whole[3].

Should my baby eat at the same times as the family?

When you can, yes. Eating together helps babies learn by copying, and by around 10 to 12 months many babies’ meals line up with breakfast, lunch, and dinner[1].
