How much does it cost to feed a family of four?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

According to USDA’s October 2026 food plan costs, a family of four with two adults and two school-age children would spend about $1,025 a month on groceries on the Thrifty plan, $1,129 on Low-Cost, $1,393 on Moderate-Cost, and $1,685 on Liberal — assuming every meal and snack is made at home[1].

USDA food plan costs for a family of four (October 2026)

Every month, USDA publishes what it would cost to buy a healthy diet at four price levels. The figures below are for its reference family: a man and a woman aged 20–50 and two children aged 6–8 and 9–11[1].

With younger kids, the number is lower

Small children eat less, so a family with a preschooler and a toddler spends less than one with two school-age kids. Using the same October 2026 per-person figures for two adults aged 20–50 and children aged 2–3 and 4–5[1]:

What these numbers do and don’t include

  • They cover food and drinks bought at grocery stores and prepared at home — not restaurants, takeout, or school lunches[1].
  • They are national averages. USDA publishes separate Thrifty costs for Alaska and Hawaii, where the reference family’s monthly cost was $1,303.90 in Anchorage and $1,582.80 in Hawaii[1].
  • Costs are updated monthly for inflation using the Consumer Price Index; monthly figures are weekly costs multiplied by 4.333[1].
  • The Thrifty plan is the basis for maximum SNAP benefits. The other three plans are older (2007) market baskets updated for prices[1].

How to estimate your own family’s cost

  1. Look up each family member’s age and sex group in USDA’s current monthly report and pick the plan closest to how you shop[1].
  2. Add up the costs for everyone in the household.
  3. Adjust for household size: USDA suggests adding 5% for a 3-person household, subtracting 5% for 5 or 6 people, and subtracting 10% for 7 or more[1].
  4. Compare the result with what you actually spend for a month, including takeout, to see where the gap is.

Which plan is closest to your family?

The four plans describe how a family shops, not how well it eats — all four are designed as healthy diets[1]. As a rough guide (our interpretation, not USDA’s definitions):

  • Thrifty: mostly cooking from scratch, store brands, minimal convenience foods, very little waste.
  • Low-Cost: home cooking with some convenience items and a bit more variety.
  • Moderate-Cost: more pre-cut, pre-made, and brand-name foods, and more meat and fresh produce.
  • Liberal: plenty of premium ingredients, specialty items, and convenience foods.

If your actual spending is well above the Liberal figure, takeout, snacks, and food waste are the usual places to look.

Ways to bring your number down

  • Plan meals and shop from a list — planning ahead is the core of budget-friendly shopping[2].
  • Swap in beans, eggs, and nuts for some meat-based meals[2].
  • Compare unit prices and try store brands[2].
  • Buy meat on sale in larger amounts and cook it for two or more meals[2].
  • Turn leftovers into lunches or new dinners instead of throwing them out[2].

Frequently asked questions

What is a realistic grocery budget for a family of four?

Based on USDA’s October 2026 figures, roughly $1,025 to $1,685 a month for two adults and two school-age kids, depending on how economically you shop[1]. Families with toddlers and preschoolers spend somewhat less.

How much does it cost to feed a toddler per month?

On USDA’s Thrifty plan, about $114 a month for a 1-year-old and $173 for a 2- to 3-year-old as of October 2026[1].

Do these numbers include eating out?

No. All four USDA food plans assume meals and snacks are prepared at home[1]. Takeout and restaurant meals come on top.

How often does USDA update these costs?

Monthly. USDA adjusts each plan for food price changes using the Consumer Price Index[1], so check the latest report for current figures.
