When can babies have cow’s milk?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

At 12 months. Before then, cow’s milk lacks enough iron and other nutrients babies need, can stress immature kidneys, and can irritate the gut[1][2]. From 12 to 24 months, offer about 16 ounces (2 cups) of whole milk a day[3]. Small amounts in cooking, plus yogurt and cheese, can come earlier[4][5].

Why wait until 12 months

  • Too little iron and vitamin C. Cow’s milk lacks the proper amounts of iron, vitamin C, and other nutrients infants need[1].
  • Hard on the kidneys. Its high protein and mineral levels can stress a young baby’s kidneys[1][2].
  • Gut irritation. Cow’s milk protein can irritate the lining of the stomach and intestine, which can cause blood loss and sometimes iron-deficiency anemia[1][2].

Until 12 months, breast milk or formula should remain your baby’s main drink. Dairy alternatives like oat or almond milk also shouldn’t be given as a drink before 12 months[2].

What’s okay before 12 months

  • Cow’s milk used in cooking or mixed into food, from around 6 months[4].
  • Plain yogurt and cheese without added sugar, as part of solid foods[5].

How much milk after 12 months

AgeTypeAmount per day
12–24 monthsWhole milk[3]About 16 oz (2 cups)[3]
2–5 yearsLow-fat or nonfat[3]16–24 oz (2–3 cups)[3]

More is not better. The AAP suggests limiting milk to about 16 ounces a day after the first birthday, and notes that more than 24 ounces a day has been linked to iron deficiency in toddlers who aren’t getting enough iron-rich food[1].

Which milk?

Whole, vitamin D–fortified milk is recommended for most children from age 1[1]. Your pediatrician may suggest 2% milk if your child is at risk for overweight or has a family history of obesity, high blood pressure, or heart disease, but 1% and nonfat milk aren’t recommended before age 2[1].

If your family doesn’t use dairy, fortified soy milk is considered nutritionally equivalent to cow’s milk[3]. Most other plant milks are lower in protein and other nutrients[3]; if you use one, choose an unflavored, unsweetened version fortified with calcium and vitamin D, and talk to your pediatrician[2].

A sample day after the switch

  • Breakfast: oatmeal with fruit and a ½ cup of whole milk in a cup.
  • Snack: cheese cubes and soft pear; water.
  • Lunch: egg or bean wrap with soft vegetables; ½ cup milk.
  • Snack: yogurt and banana; water.
  • Dinner: family meal with an iron-rich protein; ½ cup milk.

That adds up to about 1½ cups of milk from the cup, plus yogurt and cheese — comfortably within the recommended amount[3].

Common transition problems

  • Drinking too much: if your toddler wants milk all day, offer it only with meals and water in between.
  • Refusing it cold: try it at room temperature at first.
  • Attached to the bottle: offer milk in an open or straw cup with meals.
  • Constipation or tummy upset: ask your pediatrician, especially if it continues.
  • Milk allergy or intolerance in the family: talk to your pediatrician before starting.

How to make the switch

  1. Around the first birthday, start offering whole milk in an open or straw cup with meals.
  2. Replace formula feeds gradually over a week or two if your child prefers a slower change — formula isn’t needed after 12 months[4].
  3. Keep milk to about 2 cups a day and offer water between meals[3].
  4. Make sure meals include iron-rich foods, since milk is low in iron.
  5. If you’re breastfeeding, you can continue; talk to your pediatrician about how cow’s milk fits in.

Frequently asked questions

Can my 11-month-old have cow’s milk?

Not as a drink. Wait until 12 months[2]. Milk in cooking and yogurt or cheese are fine before then[4][5].

Do toddlers need toddler formula after 12 months?

Most don’t. The AAP says toddler milks are unnecessary and more expensive than regular milk[3].

What if my baby refuses cow’s milk?

Try it in a cup at meals, slightly warmed, or mixed with breast milk or formula at first. Yogurt and cheese also provide dairy. If refusal continues, ask your pediatrician.

Can I give my 1-year-old oat or almond milk instead?

Fortified soy milk is an acceptable alternative[3]. Most other plant milks aren’t recommended as a replacement for dairy because they’re lower in key nutrients[3]; ask your pediatrician first.

Is goat’s milk a good alternative before 12 months?

No. The AAP advises against cow’s milk or other non-human milk or milk substitutes until about 12 months unless no alternative is available[1].
