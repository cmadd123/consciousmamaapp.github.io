Picky eating

What can I make for dinner for a picky eater?

Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Short answer

Build dinner around one food your child reliably eats, then serve family food alongside it in small portions[1]. Build-your-own meals — tacos, pasta bars, grain bowls, sheet-pan dinners with dips — let kids choose what goes on their plate. Keep re-offering new foods; many children need 8 to 10 or more tries[2].

What makes a dinner picky-eater friendly

  • A "safe" food on the table. Every meal includes something your child usually eats — rice, bread, pasta, fruit, cheese — so they never face a plate of only unfamiliar foods[1].
  • Components, not casseroles. Many picky eaters prefer foods separate rather than mixed. Serve sauces and toppings on the side.
  • Choice within limits. Letting a child pick between two vegetables or build their own plate gives them control without a separate meal[1][2].
  • Dips. Toddlers often enjoy anything with a dip — yogurt dip, hummus, ketchup, or a mild sauce[1].
  • Familiar paired with new. The AAP suggests pairing unfamiliar or bitter foods with familiar ones, like broccoli with grated cheese[1].

For children under 4, cut foods into small pieces (no larger than about ½ inch), chop round foods like grapes and hot dogs completely, and avoid nuts, popcorn, and chunks of raw vegetables, which are choking hazards[4].

What to do

  1. Pick dinners where the base is something your child already eats, then rotate what goes with it.
  2. Put a small portion of the new food on the plate — one or two bites — with no pressure to taste it[1].
  3. Eat the new food yourself, visibly and without commentary. Modeling helps[2].
  4. Use "food bridges": once a food is accepted, offer something similar in color, flavor, or texture (e.g., sweet potato fries after regular fries)[1].
  5. Change the form, not just the food. A child who rejects cooked carrots may eat them raw and grated[3].
  6. Involve your child: let them choose a vegetable at the store or help stir, pour, or wash produce[1].
  7. Re-offer refused foods regularly. If a food isn’t accepted, wait about a week and try again[2].

Frequently asked questions

Should I cook a separate meal for my picky eater?

The AAP recommends serving one meal for the whole family and including at least one food your child likes, rather than making a replacement meal when they refuse[1].

Is hiding vegetables in food a good idea?

Blending vegetables into sauces or meatballs is fine for nutrition, but children also need to see and taste vegetables as themselves to learn to accept them. Do both: hidden in some dishes, visible on the plate in small amounts.

How many times should I offer a new food?

Many young children need 8 to 10 or more exposures before they accept a new food[2], and the AAP notes some need 10 or more tastes[1]. Keep portions tiny so repeats don’t feel wasteful.

Should I reward my child for trying new foods?

Avoid food rewards like dessert for eating vegetables — it makes the treat more exciting and the vegetable a chore. Praise and non-food rewards work better[1][3].
