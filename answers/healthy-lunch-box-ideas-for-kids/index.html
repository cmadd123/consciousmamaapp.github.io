What should I pack in my kid’s lunch box?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Aim for a protein, a grain or starch, and a fruit or vegetable — the same building blocks as a balanced snack[1] — plus water or milk. Pack it safely: an insulated lunch box with a frozen ice pack, a preheated thermos for hot food, and no more than two hours without refrigeration for perishable foods[2].

The lunch box formula

PartIdeas
ProteinTurkey or chicken, hard-boiled egg, cheese, hummus, beans, tofu, yogurt, nut or seed butter (if school allows)
Grain or starchWhole-grain bread or wrap, crackers, pasta, rice, a mini bagel
FruitApple slices, berries, orange segments, banana, grapes (cut up for young kids)
VegetableCucumber, snap peas, cherry tomatoes (quartered for young kids), leftover cooked vegetables
DrinkWater or milk

Making your own packable lunches from healthier ingredients is usually cheaper and more nutritious than prepackaged lunch kits[3].

15 lunch box ideas

  • Cold-cut roll-ups: turkey and cheese in a whole-wheat tortilla[3]
  • Cold leftover pizza with cucumber slices[3]
  • Cracker "sandwiches" with cheese and apple slices[3]
  • Peanut butter with celery sticks, if allowed at school[3]
  • Veggie sticks with dip, a cheese stick, and crackers[3]
  • Pasta salad with chicken and peas (keep it cold)
  • Hummus, pita, cucumber, and grapes
  • Hard-boiled egg, toast fingers, and berries
  • Thermos of last night’s soup or chili with bread
  • Thermos of mac and cheese with peas
  • Bean and cheese quesadilla wedges with salsa
  • Rice bowl with leftover chicken and edamame
  • Mini muffins, yogurt, and an orange
  • Pinwheel wraps with cream cheese and spinach
  • Breakfast-for-lunch: a mini bagel, cream cheese, and fruit

Keeping lunch safe until lunchtime

  • Use an insulated lunch box, and always add a frozen ice pack — even in an insulated bag[2].
  • A frozen juice box or water bottle can double as an ice pack[2].
  • Lunches with perishable foods shouldn’t go more than two hours without refrigeration[2].
  • For hot food, fill the thermos with boiling water first to preheat it, then empty and fill it with hot food[2].
  • When there’s no fridge, shelf-stable options include trail mix, cereal, granola bars, bagels, nuts, and seeds[2].
  • Wash lunch boxes daily[2][3].

More quick answers

  • Can I pack yesterday’s dinner? Yes, if it was refrigerated promptly. Pack cold leftovers with an ice pack, or reheat food until steaming and pack it in a preheated thermos[2].
  • What drink should go in a lunch box? Water or milk are the best everyday options. A frozen water bottle can double as an ice pack[2].

Plan lunches with dinners

The easiest lunches come from the dinner plan. Cook extra pasta, rice, or chicken at dinner and pack it the next morning. Involving kids helps too: let them choose between two options, and look over the school menu together[3].

For preschoolers and toddlers, cut food to a safe size. The AAP lists whole grapes, raw vegetable sticks, and chunks of meat or cheese among the foods to avoid or modify for young children[4].

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep my kid’s lunch cold without a fridge?

Use an insulated lunch box with a frozen ice pack, or freeze a water bottle or juice box to serve as one[2].

How do I keep food hot in a thermos?

Preheat the thermos with boiling water, pour it out, then add food that’s steaming hot[2].

What if my child doesn’t eat their lunch?

Pack smaller portions with at least one food they reliably eat, and involve them in choosing[3]. Throw away perishable leftovers rather than reusing them.

Are prepackaged lunch kits a good option?

They’re convenient but can be expensive and less nutritious than a lunch you assemble yourself from similar ingredients[3].

How can I get my child involved in packing lunch?

Offer two choices from each part of the formula and let them pick, or have older kids pack their own from a set of approved options. Reviewing the cafeteria menu together helps too[3].
