How much water should a baby or toddler drink?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Babies under 6 months generally don’t need water. From about 6 months, offer 4 to 8 ounces (½ to 1 cup) a day in a cup; from 12 to 24 months, 8 to 32 ounces (1 to 4 cups); and from 2 to 5 years, 8 to 40 ounces (1 to 5 cups)[1]. Hot weather and activity increase needs.

Babies under 6 months

Breast milk or formula gives young babies the fluid they need[1]. The NHS notes that fully breastfed babies don’t need water before starting solids, while formula-fed babies under 6 months may need small sips of cooled boiled water in hot weather[2]. Ask your pediatrician before giving water to a young baby.

Starting water around 6 months

When your baby starts solids, begin offering small amounts of water with meals in an open, sippy, or straw cup[1]. The NHS recommends introducing a cup from around 6 months and says an open cup or free-flow cup without a valve helps babies learn to sip[2]. Keep it modest: the AAP suggests limiting water to no more than 1 cup (8 ounces) a day when starting solids[3], so it doesn’t replace milk.

Why water is the go-to drink for toddlers

After the first birthday, water and plain milk are the drinks the AAP recommends. It advises against flavored milks, toddler milks, sugary drinks like soda and fruit drinks, drinks sweetened with stevia or artificial sweeteners, and caffeine[1].

  • Offer water between meals and with snacks.
  • Keep milk mostly at mealtimes, so it doesn’t fill your toddler up.
  • Avoid big cups of milk or juice right before a meal, which can blunt appetite[4].
  • Bring a water bottle on outings and offer more in hot weather or after active play.

Hot weather and illness

Children need more fluids when it’s hot, when they’re very active, or when they’re sick with fever, vomiting, or diarrhea. Offer water more often in those situations, and keep breastfeeding or formula feeding babies as usual. Call your pediatrician if your child can’t keep fluids down, has very few wet diapers, or seems unusually drowsy.

Making water easy to drink

  • Let your toddler choose their own cup or bottle color.
  • Keep a cup within reach during play, not just at meals.
  • Add slices of cucumber, strawberry, or orange for flavor instead of sweeteners.
  • Offer cold water from the fridge in hot weather.
  • Model it: drink water at meals yourself.

Avoid filling the gap with juice. The AAP says whole fruit is always preferred to drinking juice[1].

What to do

  1. Put a small cup of water at your child’s place at every meal and snack.
  2. Use an open or straw cup rather than a bottle from around 6 months[2].
  3. Plan drinks along with meals: milk with two or three meals, water the rest of the time.
  4. Call your pediatrician if your child shows signs of dehydration, such as very few wet diapers, a dry mouth, or unusual sleepiness.

Frequently asked questions

Can I give my newborn water?

Generally no — breast milk or formula provides what young babies need[1]. Talk to your pediatrician before giving water to a baby under 6 months[2].

Does milk count toward my toddler’s water?

Milk provides fluid, but the AAP lists water and milk separately, with distinct amounts for each[1]. Aim for both.

Is flavored water okay for toddlers?

The AAP advises avoiding drinks with added sugar or with stevia or artificial sweeteners, even if they’re calorie-free[1]. Plain water, perhaps with a slice of fruit, is the better choice.

Do I need to boil water for my baby?

The NHS says drinking water for babies over 6 months doesn’t need to be boiled[2]. That advice is for UK mains water — if you use well water or are unsure, ask your pediatrician.

Should I wake my toddler to give water?

No. Offer water regularly through the day, especially in hot weather and after active play, and let your toddler drink to thirst.
