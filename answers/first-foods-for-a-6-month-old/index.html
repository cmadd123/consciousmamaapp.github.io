Starting solids

What are the best first foods for a 6-month-old?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

There’s no required order for most babies[1]. Good first foods include iron-rich options — puréed meat, iron-fortified cereal, beans, lentils, tofu[2][3] — plus soft-cooked vegetables and fruit. Start with 1 or 2 tablespoons of smooth food and keep breast milk or formula as your baby’s main source of nutrition until 12 months[4].

Is your baby ready?

Most babies can start solids at about 6 months; starting before 4 months isn’t recommended[1]. Look for these signs[1][2]:

  • Sits up, with or without support, with good head and neck control.
  • Opens their mouth when food comes near and seems interested in what you’re eating.
  • Swallows food instead of pushing it back out with the tongue.

Pair plant iron sources with vitamin C–rich foods like broccoli or fruit to help absorption[3].

How much and how often

Start small — 1 or 2 tablespoons of food[4], or even half a spoonful or less at the very first meals[2]. At first, babies don’t need three meals a day[5]; from about 7 months they gradually move toward three, and by around 10 months most are eating three meals a day alongside their milk feeds[5].

Begin with smooth, mashed, or strained textures, then move to thicker, lumpier foods as skills develop[1]. Introduce one single-ingredient new food every 3 to 5 days so you can spot any reaction[1][2].

Mealtime safety

  • Seat your baby upright in a high chair, never reclined.
  • Stay within arm’s reach for the whole meal.
  • Offer soft foods that can be mashed between your fingers.
  • Avoid distractions like screens so your baby can focus on eating.

What to skip for now

  • Honey, until after 12 months.
  • Cow’s milk as a drink, until after 12 months[1].
  • Juice — babies under 12 months shouldn’t have it[2].
  • Added salt and sugar, including stock cubes and gravy[5].
  • Whole grapes, popcorn, nuts, and other hard or round foods that can cause choking[2].

What to do

  1. Choose a calm time when your baby isn’t overly hungry or tired.
  2. Offer an iron-rich food early on, and rotate in vegetables and fruit.
  3. Seat your baby upright and stay with them for every meal.
  4. Follow your baby’s cues. If they turn away, stop, and try again another day.
  5. Introduce common allergens like egg and peanut alongside other foods; talk to your doctor first if your baby has severe eczema or an egg allergy[1].

Frequently asked questions

Should rice cereal be my baby’s first food?

It doesn’t have to be. CDC suggests offering a variety of fortified infant cereals such as oat, barley, and multigrain instead of only rice cereal[1].

Should I start with vegetables or fruit?

For most babies, there’s no required order[1]. Many parents start with single vegetables, which the NHS suggests[5], but what matters most is variety and including iron.

What if my baby refuses a new food?

Don’t force it. Wait a week or two and try again[2]. It may take 10 tries or more for a baby to get used to new foods, flavors, and textures[5].

Should I start with purées or finger foods?

Either can work. Smooth textures are easiest at first[1]; soft finger foods come in once your baby can sit up and bring things to their mouth[2].

Can my 6-month-old have water?

Small sips from a cup with meals are fine once solids start. The AAP suggests limiting water to no more than 1 cup (8 ounces) a day at this stage[2].
