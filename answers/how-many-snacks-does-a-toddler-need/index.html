Toddler portions

How many snacks does a toddler need?

Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Short answer

Most toddlers do well with two or three planned snacks a day on top of three meals[1][2]. Schedule snacks at least 2 hours before the next meal, keep portions small, and treat them as mini-meals rather than all-day grazing — that way your child arrives at the table hungry[1][3].

Why toddlers need snacks at all

Toddlers have small stomachs and high energy needs for their size, so three meals alone often aren’t enough. The AAP notes that three meals plus 2 or 3 healthy snacks a day help children meet their nutrition needs[1]. The NHS suggests limiting snacks to two healthy ones a day between meals[2]. Either way, the point is planned snacks — not a constant supply.

The trouble starts when snacks are unplanned: a cracker in the stroller, a pouch in the car, a cup of milk on the go. Grazing all day blunts hunger, and the result is often a toddler who refuses dinner. The AAP suggests scheduled meals and limited snacks so children come to the table hungry[3].

(Optional)Bedtime snackBanana slices or a small cup of milk, if part of your routine

Portions above follow AAP toddler serving sizes — for example, ⅓ cup of yogurt, ½ oz of cheese, or ½ piece of fruit counts as one serving[4]. Shift the times to match your child’s naps and wake-up; what matters is the 2–3 hour rhythm and a gap of at least 2 hours before meals[1].

What makes a good toddler snack

Think of a snack as a small meal: pair two food groups when you can, such as a fruit or vegetable with a protein or dairy food.

A snack portion should be about one toddler serving — a few bites, not a bowl[4]. Keeping a short list of five or six go-to snacks saves deciding on the spot and makes it easier to say "snack is at 3:00" with confidence.

  • Soft fruit pieces with plain yogurt
  • Whole-grain crackers with cheese (cut into small pieces)
  • Hummus with soft-cooked vegetable sticks or pita
  • Hard-boiled egg, chopped
  • Toast fingers with a thin layer of nut butter
  • Avocado mashed on toast
  • Cottage cheese with diced peaches

For children under 4, skip common choking hazards at snack time too — popcorn, whole nuts, whole grapes, and raw carrot sticks[5].

What to do

  1. Pick fixed snack times and stick to them most days[1][3].
  2. Serve snacks sitting down, at the table or in a high chair, rather than on the move[5].
  3. End snacks at least 2 hours before meals[1].
  4. Offer water between meals. Keep juice to no more than about 4 oz of 100% juice a day for toddlers, and skip it entirely before age 1[6].
  5. Don’t use snacks as rewards or to end tantrums — the AAP advises against bribing with treats[3].
  6. If your child refuses a meal, offer the next snack at its usual time rather than right away.

Frequently asked questions

How many snacks should a 2-year-old have?

Two or three planned snacks a day, in addition to three meals, is typical[1][2].

How long should there be between a snack and a meal?

The AAP suggests at least 2 hours between snacks and meals, so children are hungry enough to eat well at mealtimes[1].

Is milk a snack?

Milk is filling, so count it. Toddlers 12 to 24 months generally need about 2 cups of milk a day in total[6]; large amounts between meals can crowd out food.

Should I give my toddler a snack if they didn’t eat their meal?

Not as an immediate replacement. Offer the next planned snack at its usual time. This keeps meals meaningful without letting your child go hungry for long.

Are packaged toddler snacks okay?

Occasionally, as long as you check labels for added sugar and salt. Treat them like any snack — at a set time, sitting down — and skip gummy or sticky ones for young children, which can be choking hazards[5].
