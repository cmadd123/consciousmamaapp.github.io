What are the best freezer meals to make before baby arrives?

Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Soups, stews, chili, casseroles, baked pasta, and breakfast burritos freeze well and reheat easily[1]. Cool food quickly in small containers, label it with the date, and aim to eat most frozen meals within two to three months for best quality[2]. Skip foods that freeze poorly, like lettuce, mayonnaise, and cottage cheese[1].

12 freezer meals that work for new parents

The best postpartum meals can be eaten with one hand, reheat from a single container, and don’t need a side dish.

  1. Turkey or bean chili
  2. Chicken and vegetable soup
  3. Beef or lentil stew
  4. Baked ziti or lasagna
  5. Chicken enchiladas
  6. Shepherd’s pie
  7. Burritos with beans, rice, and cheese, wrapped individually
  8. Egg, potato, and cheese breakfast burritos
  9. Meatballs in tomato sauce
  10. Mac and cheese with peas
  11. Banana or zucchini muffins
  12. Oat and nut-butter energy bars

Freezing and reheating safely

  • Cool food fast. Portion it into smaller containers and refrigerate right away rather than leaving a big pot on the counter[1][2].
  • Refrigerate or freeze within 2 hours of cooking (1 hour if it’s above 90°F)[3].
  • Keep your freezer at 0°F or below[3].
  • Label every container with the dish and the date[2].
  • Thaw in the refrigerator, in cold water, or in the microwave — not on the counter[3].
  • Reheat to 165°F, checked with a food thermometer[3].

Frozen food stays safe much longer than refrigerated food[1], but quality fades with time. Many leftovers are best within two to three months in the freezer[2].

Plan for feeding yourself, too

If you’re breastfeeding, CDC notes that well-nourished breastfeeding mothers need an additional 330 to 400 calories a day compared with before pregnancy[5]. Generally, you don’t need to avoid specific foods while breastfeeding, though some fish should be limited because of mercury[5].

  • Freeze snacks as well as dinners: muffins, burritos, and bars.
  • Make some meals in single portions for the middle-of-the-night hunger.
  • Keep a list of what’s in the freezer on the door so partners and helpers can pick something.
  • Plan a simple rotation for the first few weeks so nobody has to think about dinner.

A simple freezer-prep plan

  • Weeks 1–2: double two regular dinners each week and freeze the second batch.
  • Week 3: one batch-cooking session — chili, soup, and a pasta bake.
  • Week 4: breakfast burritos and muffins, wrapped individually.
  • Last week: stock the pantry with quick backups such as pasta, jarred sauce, oats, canned beans, and frozen vegetables.

Write each meal on a freezer inventory list as you go, and cross it off when it’s eaten. Use the oldest packages first[4].

More quick answers

  • How do I reheat a frozen casserole safely? Thaw it in the refrigerator overnight, or use the microwave’s defrost setting, then reheat until it reaches 165°F in the center[3]. Reheat only what you’ll eat.

How many meals to make

There’s no right number. Many families aim for enough dinners to cover two to four weeks, plus breakfasts and snacks, then fill gaps with meal trains or simple pantry meals. Start about a month or two before your due date and cook double batches of regular dinners — one for tonight, one for the freezer[4].

Frequently asked questions

How long do freezer meals last?

Frozen food stays safe for a long time, but for best quality, plan to eat most frozen leftovers within two to three months[2].

Do I need a special diet while breastfeeding?

Generally no. CDC says most breastfeeding mothers don’t need to limit or avoid specific foods, but they do need more energy and certain nutrients, and should limit higher-mercury fish[5]. Ask your health care provider about supplements.

What containers are best for freezer meals?

Use freezer-safe containers or bags in meal-sized portions. Flat bags thaw faster and stack neatly. Label everything with the date[2].

Can I freeze meals with dairy or eggs?

Cooked dishes like mac and cheese, lasagna, and breakfast burritos freeze well. Avoid freezing eggs in the shell, cottage cheese, and mayonnaise-based dishes[1].

What are good freezer meals for a breastfeeding mom?

Any balanced meal works. Choose ones with protein, whole grains, and vegetables — chili with beans, lentil stew, chicken and vegetable soup — plus easy snacks, since breastfeeding raises energy needs[5].
