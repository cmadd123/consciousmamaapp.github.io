High Chair vs. Booster Seat

Which feeding seat your child needs — and when to move from one to the other.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

A high chair is a standalone seat with its own tray, made for babies starting solids (around 6 months) who can sit up with support[1]. A booster seat straps onto a regular dining chair and suits older toddlers (roughly 18 months and up) who can sit at the table. Most families use a high chair first and move to a booster later; some convertible seats do both. The right pick depends on your child's age, your space, and whether you want them eating at the family table.

High chair

A standalone seat with a tray, made for babies ~6 months to ~3 years. More supportive and adjustable, but takes floor space and costs more.

Booster seat

Straps to a normal dining chair for toddlers who can sit at the table. Cheaper, portable, and brings the child to family meals — but offers less recline/support for young babies.

Which should you choose?

  • Your baby is just starting solidsHigh chair
  • Small kitchen or lots of travelBooster or clip-on seat

Frequently asked questions

When can a baby use a high chair?

Once they can sit upright with support and have good head control — usually around 6 months, the same readiness window as starting solids[1]. Always use the safety straps, including the crotch strap; never let your child stand in the chair; and keep it away from counters and tables they could push off from[2].

When should we switch to a booster?

Many toddlers move to a booster around 18 months to 3 years, once they can sit steadily at the table and follow basic mealtime routines.

Are clip-on / hook-on boosters safe?

The AAP says hook-on seats aren't a good replacement for a freestanding high chair. If you use one when eating out or traveling, pick one that locks onto the table, make sure the table is heavy and stable enough to hold your child without tipping, and check that your child's feet can't push off a table support[2]. Follow the manufacturer's weight limits.

Do I need both?

Not necessarily. Some families use a high chair first then a booster; others buy a convertible seat that adjusts from one to the other to skip owning both.
