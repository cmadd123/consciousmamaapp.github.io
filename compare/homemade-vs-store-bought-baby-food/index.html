Homemade vs. Store-Bought Baby Food

Cost, nutrition, convenience, and safety — the honest trade-offs.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

Both can be healthy. Homemade baby food can be less expensive[1], lets you control ingredients and textures, and can expose babies to more variety and family flavors — but it takes time and requires safe prep and storage. Store-bought is convenient, consistent, shelf-stable, and portable, though it costs more per serving and offers less texture variety. Many families do a mix. Whichever you choose, skip added salt and sugar and follow allergen-introduction guidance[3].

Homemade

You cook and purée (or mash) it yourself. Cheaper, full ingredient control, more texture and flavor variety — but takes time and safe storage.

Store-bought

Pre-made pouches and jars. Convenient, consistent, shelf-stable, and portable — at a higher cost per serving and with less texture variety.

Frequently asked questions

Is homemade baby food healthier?

It can be, because you control ingredients and can avoid added salt and sugar — but many store-bought options are also nutritious. The bigger factors are variety, texture progression, and no added salt/sugar, which either can deliver.

How do I store homemade baby food safely?

Refrigerate in a covered container and use within 1–2 days, or freeze in small portions (ice-cube trays hold about 1 oz each) labeled with the date[1][2]. For best quality, freezer guidance ranges from 1–2 months[2] up to 3 months[1]. Refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours[4], and throw out any food left in the dish your baby ate from[2].

Are pouches bad for babies?

Pouches are fine in moderation. Squeezing them onto a spoon gives your baby spoon practice, and moving on to lumpier and finger-food textures as soon as your baby can manage them helps them learn to chew[5].

What should I avoid in baby food?

No honey before age 1[1], no added salt[3] or sugar, and introduce common allergens one at a time[3]. Watch textures and shapes for choking, and check with your pediatrician.
