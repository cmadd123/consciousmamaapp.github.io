Homemade vs. Store-Bought Baby Food

Cost, nutrition, convenience, and safety — the honest trade-offs.

Quick answer

Both can be healthy. Homemade baby food is usually cheaper, lets you control ingredients and textures, and can expose babies to more variety and family flavors — but it takes time and requires safe prep and storage. Store-bought is convenient, consistent, shelf-stable, and portable, though it costs more per serving and offers less texture variety. Many families do a mix. Whichever you choose, skip added salt and sugar and follow allergen-introduction guidance.

Homemade

You cook and purée (or mash) it yourself. Cheaper, full ingredient control, more texture and flavor variety — but takes time and safe storage.

Store-bought

Pre-made pouches and jars. Convenient, consistent, shelf-stable, and portable — at a higher cost per serving and with less texture variety.

Frequently asked questions

Is homemade baby food healthier?

It can be, because you control ingredients and can avoid added salt and sugar — but many store-bought options are also nutritious. The bigger factors are variety, texture progression, and no added salt/sugar, which either can deliver.

How do I store homemade baby food safely?

Refrigerate and use within a couple of days, or freeze in small portions (ice-cube trays work well) and use within a month. Cool cooked food quickly and don't refreeze thawed purée.

Are pouches bad for babies?

Pouches are fine in moderation, but sucking straight from the spout too often can limit spoon and chewing skills and let sugary fruit blends pool on teeth. Spoon pouch contents out when you can.

What should I avoid in baby food?

No honey before age 1, no added salt or sugar, and introduce common allergens one at a time. Watch textures/shapes for choking, and check with your pediatrician.

General information, not medical or food-safety advice. Follow safe food-prep and storage guidance and your pediatrician's recommendations, especially for allergens and textures.
