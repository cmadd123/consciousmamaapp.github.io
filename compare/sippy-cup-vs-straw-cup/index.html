Sippy Cup vs. Straw Cup

What to hand your toddler when the bottle goes away — and why it matters for teeth and speech.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

Pediatric guidance favors open cups and straw cups over traditional valved, hard-spout sippy cups. The AAP suggests skipping valved sippy cups — they make a child suck much like a bottle — and working toward an open cup by about age 2[1], and the NHS notes that open or free-flow cups are better for teeth[2]. Frequent sipping of milk or juice from any bottle or cup raises the risk of cavities[1]. Spout cups are still handy for spill control; many families use a straw or open cup for everyday drinking and keep a spill-resistant cup for the car or stroller.

Sippy cup (hard spout)

Spill-resistant and an easy step from the bottle. But valved spouts make a child suck much like a bottle, and sipping milk or juice from one throughout the day is hard on teeth[1].

Straw cup

A common next step that sits closer to open-cup drinking, which the AAP suggests working toward[1]. Can be spill-resistant, though it takes a little practice to learn.

Frequently asked questions

When should we switch from a bottle to a cup?

Common guidance is to introduce a cup around 6 months, when solids start, and aim to finish moving off the bottle between 12 and 18 months[1][2]. Starting a straw or open cup early makes the transition smoother.

Are hard-spout sippy cups bad?

They're fine as a short learning step, but the AAP suggests avoiding valved sippy cups because they make a child suck like a bottle, and all-day sipping of milk or juice from any cup raises cavity risk[1]. Straw and open cups are better for daily drinking.

What about open cups?

Open cups (with help) are where guidance points — the NHS suggests offering sips of water from an open or free-flow cup with meals from around 6 months[2]. Expect spills while your child learns.

What's a good age for a straw cup?

There's no official age — some babies pick up a straw within the first year, others a bit later. It's fine to introduce one as soon as your child shows interest.
