Best Meal Planning Apps for Moms and Families (2026)

Nine family meal planners compared on price, devices, grocery lists, recipe import, kid features and sharing, with a pick for each kind of family.

Quick answer

No single meal planning app is best for every family. The right one depends on what you need most. Based on each app's published features and prices (checked November 26, 2026): Cozi is the best free option and family organizer. Plan to Eat and Paprika are best if you plan from recipes you already collect. Feeding Littles is built for babies and toddlers starting solids, and Little Lunches for picky eaters and lunchboxes. eMeals writes the weekly menu for you, Eat This Much plans around nutrition targets, and Ollie does it all by text. MomRise, our app, suits iPhone families who want meal planning, a family calendar, milestones and activities in one place. It has no free plan after its 7-day trial and no Android version.

Looking for Mealime? Mealime was discontinued on October 21, 2026, and its recipes moved to Albertsons' Meals Hub[1]. See Mealime alternatives.

The apps, one by one

Best free option and best family organizer

Cozi Family Organizer

Who it's for: Families whose main problem is coordination — one shared calendar, shopping list and to-do list for everyone — with meal planning as one part of it.

Strengths

  • Free plan includes the meal planner, recipe box, shared grocery lists and shared calendar
  • Everyone in the family signs in to the same account
  • Works on iPhone, Android and the web
  • Planned meals show up on the family calendar

Drawbacks

  • Ingredients go to the list recipe by recipe rather than from the whole week at once
  • The free plan isn't ad-free (ad-free is a Gold feature)
  • AI meal planning requires the $79/yr Max plan
  • No picky-eater or baby-feeding features

Price: Free; Cozi Gold $39/yr (ad-free); Cozi Max $79/yr (adds AI meal planner) · Platforms: iPhone, Android, web · Checked November 26, 2026[2][3][4] · Official site

Best for planning from recipes you already save

Plan to Eat

Who it's for: Families who already have favorite recipes scattered across blogs and social media and want to plan the week from their own collection, on any device.

Strengths

  • Built around your own recipes: clip from websites and social media, or import from a photo
  • Grocery list comes straight from the plan and is organized by store and aisle
  • Works on iPhone, Android and the web
  • One account can be shared across the household

Drawbacks

  • No free plan after the trial
  • No kid-, baby- or picky-eater-specific features listed
  • It's a planner for your recipes, not a source of ready-made plans

Price: 14-day free trial, then $5.95/mo or $49/yr (website prices; in-app prices can differ) · Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android, web · Checked November 26, 2026[5][6] · Official site

Best recipe manager across devices, including Mac and Windows

Paprika Recipe Manager

Who it's for: Recipe collectors who want a personal cookbook with meal planning and grocery lists across the widest range of devices, including Mac and Windows.

Strengths

  • Free version with no time limit (up to 50 recipes)
  • Available on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and Windows
  • Website import on the free version; social, text and cookbook-page import with Premium
  • Premium family accounts share one subscription

Drawbacks

  • Free version is limited to 50 recipes, one device and one grocery list
  • Premium price isn't published on its website (it varies by region)
  • No kid-, baby- or picky-eater-specific features listed

Price: Free (up to 50 recipes); Premium is a subscription whose price varies by region and is shown in your app store · Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android, Windows · Checked November 26, 2026[7][8] · Official site

Best for babies and toddlers starting solids

Feeding Littles

Who it's for: Parents of babies and toddlers starting solids or baby-led weaning who want feeding-focused recipes alongside a family meal planner.

Strengths

  • Usable free plan with a planner, recipes and an automatic grocery list
  • Imports from Instagram, TikTok, websites, PDFs and screenshots
  • Premium household for up to 6 people

Drawbacks

  • Only 5 recipe imports on the free plan
  • Premium costs $9.99/mo — more than the other recipe apps here that publish a monthly price
  • No web or desktop version

Price: Free plan; Premium $9.99/mo or $59.99/yr (App Store) · Platforms: iPhone, Android. No web or desktop version. · Checked November 26, 2026[9][10] · Official site

Ollie

Who it's for: Parents who want to hand off meal planning entirely by texting an AI assistant, and don't need a recipe app.

Strengths

  • Works over text messages — nothing to install
  • Plans meals around your family and writes the grocery list
  • Can order groceries through Amazon, Instacart or Walmart
  • Partners and helpers can join the same group text

Drawbacks

  • Paid plans ($25 and $100 a month) cost more than every other app here
  • The free plan is capped by message count
  • We couldn't confirm recipe import or kid-specific features on its site

How to choose a meal planning app for your family

  • Decide who writes the menu. If you want ready-made plans, look at eMeals, Little Lunches or Ollie. If you'd rather plan from your own favorites, Plan to Eat, Paprika, Cozi and MomRise are built around saving recipes. Eat This Much generates plans from nutrition targets.
  • Check everyone's devices. If a partner uses Android or you like to plan on a laptop, rule out iPhone-only MomRise and Feeding Littles, which has no web version. Paprika also runs on Mac and Windows.
  • Look at what's free after the trial. Cozi, Paprika, Eat This Much, Feeding Littles and Ollie have free plans, each with limits. Plan to Eat, eMeals, Little Lunches and MomRise are paid once the trial ends.
  • Test recipe import with your real sources. If your recipes live on TikTok, Instagram or Pinterest, import three of them during the trial. Plan to Eat, Feeding Littles and MomRise list social-media or screenshot import, and Paprika does with Premium.
  • Match it to your kids' stage. For starting solids, try Feeding Littles or Little Lunches. For a picky eater, try Little Lunches, Feeding Littles or eMeals' Kid Friendly plan. For the feeding side, see how to meal plan for a picky eater.
  • Make sure your partner can use it. Cozi, Plan to Eat, Paprika Premium, Feeding Littles Premium and Little Lunches let a partner sign in to the same plan. Ollie uses a group text. MomRise only shares plans and recipes by link.
  • Count the grocery step. A list built from the whole week saves the most time. Cozi adds ingredients one recipe at a time, and Eat This Much's automatic list is Premium-only.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free meal planning app for families?

Cozi's free plan includes its meal planner, recipe box, shared grocery lists and family calendar [2][3][4]. Other free options have tighter limits: Paprika's free version holds up to 50 recipes on one device [7][8], Eat This Much's free plan makes one day's plan at a time [17][18][19], Feeding Littles' free plan allows 5 recipe imports [9][10], and Ollie's free plan caps messages [20][21]. MomRise has no free plan after its 7-day trial.

Which meal planning apps are best for picky eaters?

Little Lunches lists picky-eater feeding support and kid-focused weekly plans from baby to teen [11][12][13]. Feeding Littles includes picky-eating tips and baby-led-weaning-friendly recipes [9][10], and eMeals offers a Kid Friendly meal plan [15][16]. Most general planners, MomRise included, have no picky-eater-specific tools.

Can my partner and I share the same meal plan?

Yes, in several apps. Cozi and Plan to Eat share one account across the household [2][3][4][5][6]. Paprika and Feeding Littles offer family or household accounts on Premium [7][8][9][10], and Little Lunches lets a second parent or caregiver join free [11][12][13]. Ollie works through a family group text [20][21]. MomRise lets you share a week, day, meal or recipe by link but has no shared household login.

Which meal planning apps can import recipes from TikTok or Instagram?

Plan to Eat's Recipe Clipper saves from Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest [5][6]. Feeding Littles imports from Instagram, TikTok, websites, PDFs and screenshots [9][10]. Paprika imports social media posts with Premium [7][8]. MomRise imports TikTok links directly and Instagram posts from a screenshot [14].

Is MomRise available on Android?

No. As of November 2026, MomRise is iPhone-only [14]. If anyone in your household uses Android, Cozi, Plan to Eat, Paprika, eMeals, Eat This Much, Feeding Littles and Little Lunches all list Android apps.

What happened to Mealime?

Mealime was discontinued on October 21, 2026. Its owner, Albertsons Companies, moved its recipes to Meals Hub, and personal data was deleted at shutdown[1]. See our Mealime alternatives guide.
