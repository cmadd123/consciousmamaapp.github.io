Toddler Bed vs. Crib

When to make the switch — and how to know your child is ready.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

There's no single right age — the real trigger is safety. The AAP says that once your toddler can climb out of the crib, it may be time for a "big kid" bed[1], and crib-safety guidance says children should stop using a crib by about 35 inches tall (or the manufacturer's height limit)[2]. If they're still safely contained and sleeping well, there's no rush.

Crib

Enclosed and contained — keeps a young child safely in place. Works well until they can climb out or outgrow the height limit.

Toddler bed

A low, open bed (often using the crib mattress). Gives independence and easy in-and-out — but the room needs to be fully childproofed.

Side-by-side comparison

CribToddler bed
Typical timing Birth to when climbing starts Once climbing out or ~35 in tall — no fixed age[1][2]
Safety Contains the child; risk once they climb Open — requires a childproofed room + often a rail
Independence Low — needs lifting in/out High — child gets in and out alone
Night potty training Harder — can't get out Easier — child can reach the bathroom
Cost Already have it Low if it uses the crib mattress
Sleep disruption risk Stable Possible short-term regressions as they test freedom

Which should you choose?

  • Your child is climbing out of the cribSwitch to a toddler bed now (safety)
  • You're night-potty-trainingToddler bed
  • A new baby needs the cribTransition a bit earlier, ideally weeks ahead
  • Your child is content and safely containedKeep the crib — no rush

Frequently asked questions

Is it bad to switch too early?

Switching before a child understands staying in bed can cause bedtime battles and lost sleep. Unless safety (climbing) or a new sibling requires it, there's no reason to rush.

My toddler climbs out of the crib — what should I do?

Climbing is a fall risk, so it's the clearest sign to move to a toddler or low bed[1]. Keep the crib mattress at its lowest setting[2]; if they still climb, switch and childproof the room — gate the doorway and stairs and anchor furniture so it can't tip[1].

Toddler bed or floor bed?

Both are low and safe options. A floor bed (a Montessori-style mattress on the floor) offers the most freedom; a toddler bed with a rail offers a little more containment.

Do I need a bed rail?

A rail helps prevent rolling out for younger toddlers. Many families use one at first and remove it once the child sleeps reliably.

General guidance only — follow current safe-sleep recommendations and your pediatrician's advice, and always childproof the room before an open bed.
