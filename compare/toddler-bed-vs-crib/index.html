Toddler Bed vs. Crib

When to make the switch — and how to know your child is ready.

Quick answer

There's no single right age — most children move from a crib to a toddler bed sometime between about 18 months and 3.5 years. The most important trigger is safety: once your child can climb out of the crib (or reaches roughly 35 inches / the manufacturer's height limit), it's time to switch to prevent falls. If they're still safely contained and sleeping well, there's no rush.

Crib

Enclosed and contained — keeps a young child safely in place. Works well until they can climb out or outgrow the height limit.

Toddler bed

A low, open bed (often using the crib mattress). Gives independence and easy in-and-out — but the room needs to be fully childproofed.

Frequently asked questions

Is it bad to switch too early?

Switching before a child understands staying in bed can cause bedtime battles and lost sleep. Unless safety (climbing) or a new sibling requires it, many experts suggest waiting until closer to 3.

My toddler climbs out of the crib — what should I do?

Climbing is a fall risk, so it's the clearest sign to move to a toddler or low bed. Lower the mattress first if you can; if they still climb, switch and childproof the room.

Toddler bed or floor bed?

Both are low and safe options. A floor bed (a Montessori-style mattress on the floor) offers the most freedom; a toddler bed with a rail offers a little more containment.

Do I need a bed rail?

A rail helps prevent rolling out for younger toddlers. Many families use one at first and remove it once the child sleeps reliably.

General guidance only — follow current safe-sleep recommendations and your pediatrician's advice, and always childproof the room before an open bed.
