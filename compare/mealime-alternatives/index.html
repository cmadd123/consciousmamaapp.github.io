Mealime Alternatives After the Shutdown (2026)

Mealime closed on October 21, 2026. Here's what happened, what to look for in a replacement, seven alternatives, and how to move your recipes.

Quick answer

Mealime was discontinued on October 21, 2026. Its owner, Albertsons Companies, moved Mealime's recipes into Meals Hub, a meal-planning section of Albertsons Companies store apps and websites. Personal data was deleted at shutdown, and Mealime's notice doesn't mention any export[1]. If you want ready-made plans with an automatic grocery list, as Mealime offered, look at eMeals or Eat This Much. Eat This Much can also import recipes shared from Meals Hub[2]. To plan from your own recipes, try Plan to Eat or Paprika. For a free family option, try Cozi. MomRise, our app, suits iPhone families who also want milestones, activities and a family calendar.

What happened to Mealime

  • Shutdown date: Mealime was discontinued on October 21, 2026[1].
  • Owner: Albertsons Companies, which acquired Mealime in 2021[1].
  • Subscriptions: Mealime wound down its paid subscriptions and made all Pro recipes and features free for every user before the shutdown[1].
  • Recipes: Mealime's recipes continue in Meals Hub, along with more recipes. You find Meals Hub under the "More" icon in an Albertsons Companies store app, or on Albertsons Companies store websites[1].
  • Your data: Mealime said all personal data would be deleted when the app shut down, and that some preferences have to be set up again in Meals Hub. Its notice doesn't mention a way to export saved recipes, plans or lists[1].

Should you just switch to Meals Hub?

Meals Hub is Mealime's official successor, and it's supported by the same core team that built Mealime[1]. If you already shop at an Albertsons Companies store, it's the most direct continuation. We left it out of the table below because it lives inside grocery store apps, and we couldn't verify its pricing, platforms or features from a standalone listing. If you shop elsewhere or want a dedicated planner, the alternatives below are worth a look.

What to look for in a Mealime replacement

  • Who builds the plan. Mealime handed you a ready-made plan. eMeals, Eat This Much, Little Lunches and Ollie generate plans. Plan to Eat, Paprika, Cozi and MomRise are built around recipes you add.
  • A grocery list built from the whole week. Plan to Eat, eMeals, Feeding Littles and MomRise build one automatically from the plan. Eat This Much does on Premium. Cozi adds ingredients one recipe at a time.
  • Recipe import, so you can rescue your favorites. Every alternative on this list can import recipes from a website or link.
  • A free plan, if you used Mealime for free. Cozi, Paprika, Eat This Much and Feeding Littles have free plans with limits. Plan to Eat, eMeals and MomRise are paid after a trial.
  • Your dietary preferences. They didn't carry over from Mealime[1], so check that your new app supports the filters you need before you commit.

The alternatives, one by one

Best for done-for-you weekly menus, like Mealime's

eMeals

Who it's for: Busy parents who want someone else to write the weekly menu and want to send the grocery list straight to a delivery or pickup service.

Like Mealime, eMeals gives you a weekly plan and builds the shopping list for you. It adds a Kid Friendly plan and sends the list to grocery delivery and pickup services [3][4].

Strengths

  • Ready-made weekly meal plans, including a Kid Friendly plan
  • Shopping list is built for you and can go to several grocery delivery and pickup services
  • Also lets you add your own and web recipes
  • iPhone, Android and web

Drawbacks

  • No free plan listed after the trial
  • Month-to-month price isn't published on its site
  • We couldn't confirm household sharing from its own pages

Price: 14-day free trial; as low as $4.99/mo on a 12-month subscription, or $37.50 for 3 months · Platforms: iPhone, Android, web · Checked November 26, 2026[3][4] · Official site

Best for moving recipes over from Meals Hub

Eat This Much

Who it's for: Parents who plan meals around calorie or nutrition targets and want the app to generate plans that hit them.

Eat This Much publishes a switch-from-Mealime guide. It says you can share a recipe from Meals Hub in the Albertsons or Safeway app straight into Eat This Much, and that importing is free[2].

Strengths

  • Free plan generates daily meal plans
  • Lets you set different nutrition targets for each day of the week
  • Premium plans a full week and builds the grocery list, with delivery integration
  • Imports recipes shared from your browser or from Meals Hub

Drawbacks

  • Weekly planning and the automatic grocery list are Premium-only
  • Built around nutrition targets more than kids' tastes — no picky-eater features
  • We couldn't confirm household sharing from its own pages

Price: Free (one day at a time); Premium $5/mo with an annual subscription · Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android, web · Checked November 26, 2026[5][6][2] · Official site

Best for rebuilding your own recipe collection

Plan to Eat

Who it's for: Families who already have favorite recipes scattered across blogs and social media and want to plan the week from their own collection, on any device.

  • One account can be shared across the household

Drawbacks

  • No free plan after the trial
  • No kid-, baby- or picky-eater-specific features listed
  • It's a planner for your recipes, not a source of ready-made plans

Price: 14-day free trial, then $5.95/mo or $49/yr (website prices; in-app prices can differ) · Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Android, web · Checked November 26, 2026[7][8] · Official site

How to move your Mealime recipes

Mealime deleted personal data at shutdown and its notice mentions no export[1], so there's no way to bulk-move an old account. The recipes themselves live on in Meals Hub[1], which gives you a few routes:

  • Share them into Eat This Much. Eat This Much says you can share a recipe from Meals Hub in the Albertsons or Safeway app into its app[2].
  • Paste a link. If you can get a web link for a recipe, paste it into an app that imports from websites. Plan to Eat, Paprika, Cozi, eMeals, Feeding Littles and MomRise all do.
  • Use a screenshot or photo. Plan to Eat, Feeding Littles and MomRise import recipes from photos or screenshots. Paprika scans cookbook pages with Premium.
  • Copy the text. MomRise has a paste-text import, and Paprika Premium pulls recipes from copied text.
  • Re-enter your dietary preferences in the new app, since they didn't carry over[1].

Tip: move the ten recipes you cook most during the new app's free trial. That way you know import works for your recipes before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

When did Mealime shut down?

Mealime was discontinued on October 21, 2026[1]. Before that, it stopped paid subscriptions and made all Pro recipes and features free for every user[1].

What is Meals Hub?

Meals Hub is a meal-planning section inside Albertsons Companies store apps and websites. Mealime's recipes moved there, and Mealime says it's supported by the same core team that built Mealime[1].

Can I export my Mealime recipes and meal plans?

Mealime's notice doesn't mention any export, and it says all personal data would be deleted at shutdown[1]. The recipes are still available in Meals Hub[1]. Eat This Much says you can share Meals Hub recipes into its app[2], and other apps can import a recipe from a link, screenshot or copied text.

Which app is most like Mealime?

If what you liked was a ready-made weekly plan with an automatic grocery list, eMeals [3][4] and Eat This Much [5][6][2] work most like Mealime did. If you mainly used Mealime as a recipe box, Plan to Eat or Paprika will feel more natural.

Is there a free Mealime alternative?

Cozi's free plan includes a meal planner, recipe box and shared grocery lists [11][12][13]. Paprika's free version holds up to 50 recipes [9][10]. Eat This Much's free plan makes one day's plan at a time [5][6][2], and Feeding Littles has a free plan with 5 recipe imports [15][16]. MomRise is not free after its 7-day trial.

Does MomRise work on Android?

No. MomRise is iPhone-only as of November 2026 [14]. If you need Android, Cozi, Plan to Eat, Paprika, eMeals, Eat This Much and Feeding Littles all list Android apps.
