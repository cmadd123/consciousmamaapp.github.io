Baby-Led Weaning vs. Purées

The two main ways to start solids, compared side by side — and how to pick (or combine) them.

Quick answer

Baby-led weaning (BLW) means letting your baby self-feed soft, graspable pieces of food from the start, while the purée approach means spoon-feeding smooth mashed food and gradually thickening the texture. Neither is medically "better" — major pediatric groups consider both safe when done with age-appropriate textures and close supervision. Many families combine them. The right choice comes down to your baby's readiness, your comfort with mess and gagging, and what fits your routine.

Baby-led weaning (BLW)

Baby feeds themselves soft finger foods from the beginning — no purée stage. You offer appropriately sized, soft pieces and let them explore, pick up, and eat at their own pace.

Purées (spoon-feeding)

You spoon-feed smooth purées first, then move to mashed and lumpier textures, and finally to finger foods. The caregiver controls pace and portion early on.

Side-by-side comparison

Baby-led weaning (BLW)Purées (spoon-feeding)
How baby eats Self-feeds whole soft pieces by hand Caregiver spoon-feeds smooth food
Typical start Around 6 months, once baby can sit up with little support, holds their head steady, and reaches for food Often 4–6 months depending on readiness and pediatric guidance
Texture progression Starts at soft finger-food texture Smooth → mashed → lumpy → finger foods
Mess Higher — exploring is part of the method Lower, more contained early on
Portion control / tracking intake Harder to measure how much was eaten Easier to see quantity consumed
Gagging (normal) vs. choking Gagging is common early and is a normal protective reflex; requires close supervision and safe textures/shapes Smooth textures reduce early gagging; supervision still required as textures advance
Exposure to textures & self-regulation Early, varied texture exposure; baby sets their own pace Gradual, caregiver-paced texture exposure

Which should you choose?

  • You want your baby to self-feed and explore textures earlyBaby-led weaning
  • You want to see exactly how much your baby is eatingPurées
  • Gagging makes you anxious and you'd rather ease in graduallyPurées, then finger foods
  • You want less mess at the startPurées
  • You want the flexibility of bothA combination — many families do purées at some meals and finger foods at others

Frequently asked questions

Is baby-led weaning safe?

Pediatric guidance considers baby-led weaning safe when your baby shows readiness signs, you offer soft age-appropriate textures and shapes, and you supervise every meal. Learn the difference between gagging (normal) and choking, avoid common choking hazards, and check with your pediatrician before starting.

Which is better for picky eating later?

Evidence is mixed and no method is proven to prevent picky eating. Early, repeated exposure to a variety of flavors and textures — which either approach can provide — is the factor most often linked to broader acceptance.

Can I do both baby-led weaning and purées?

Yes. A combined or 'responsive' approach is common — for example, offering purées at some meals and soft finger foods at others. Babies can move fluidly between the two.

When should we start solids?

Most guidance points to around 6 months, and readiness signs (sitting with support, steady head control, interest in food, loss of the tongue-thrust reflex) matter more than a fixed date. Your pediatrician can confirm the right time for your baby.

What foods should we avoid at first?

Avoid honey before age 1, choking hazards (whole grapes, nuts, hard raw vegetables, chunks of meat/cheese), and added salt and sugar. Introduce common allergens one at a time and watch for reactions, per your pediatrician's guidance.

This is general educational information, not medical advice. Feeding readiness and safety vary by child — always follow your pediatrician's guidance, and learn infant choking first aid before starting solids.
