Baby-Led Weaning vs. Purées

The two main ways to start solids, compared side by side — and how to pick (or combine) them.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

Baby-led weaning (BLW) means letting your baby self-feed soft, graspable pieces of food from the start, while the purée approach means spoon-feeding smooth mashed food and gradually thickening the texture. Neither is proven "better" — the NHS, for example, says babies can start with either finger foods or smooth and mashed foods, as long as textures are age-appropriate and an adult supervises every meal[3]. Many families combine them. The right choice comes down to your baby's readiness, your comfort with mess and gagging, and what fits your routine.

Baby-led weaning (BLW)

Baby feeds themselves soft finger foods from the beginning — no purée stage. You offer appropriately sized, soft pieces and let them explore, pick up, and eat at their own pace.

Purées (spoon-feeding)

You spoon-feed smooth purées first, then move to mashed and lumpier textures, and finally to finger foods. The caregiver controls pace and portion early on.

Side-by-side comparison

Baby-led weaning (BLW)Purées (spoon-feeding)
How baby eats Self-feeds whole soft pieces by hand Caregiver spoon-feeds smooth food
Typical start Around 6 months, once baby can sit up with little support, holds their head steady, and reaches for food[3] Around 6 months for most babies, based on readiness signs — not before 4 months[1][2]
Texture progression Starts at soft finger-food texture Smooth → mashed → lumpy → finger foods
Mess Higher — exploring is part of the method Lower, more contained early on
Portion control / tracking intake Harder to measure how much was eaten Easier to see quantity consumed
Gagging (normal) vs. choking Gagging is common early and is different from choking[3]; requires close supervision and safe textures/shapes Smooth textures reduce early gagging; supervision still required as textures advance
Exposure to textures & self-regulation Early, varied texture exposure; baby sets their own pace Gradual, caregiver-paced texture exposure

Which should you choose?

  • You want your baby to self-feed and explore textures earlyBaby-led weaning
  • You want to see exactly how much your baby is eatingPurées
  • Gagging makes you anxious and you'd rather ease in graduallyPurées, then finger foods
  • You want less mess at the startPurées
  • You want the flexibility of bothA combination — many families do purées at some meals and finger foods at others

Frequently asked questions

Is baby-led weaning safe?

Baby-led weaning can be done safely when your baby shows readiness signs, you offer soft age-appropriate textures and shapes, and you supervise every meal. Learn the difference between gagging (normal) and choking, avoid common choking hazards[4], and check with your pediatrician before starting.

Which is better for picky eating later?

Evidence is mixed and no method is proven to prevent picky eating. Early, repeated exposure to a variety of flavors and textures — which either approach can provide — is what generally helps children accept a wider range of foods.

Can I do both baby-led weaning and purées?

Yes. A combined or 'responsive' approach is common — for example, offering purées at some meals and soft finger foods at others. Babies can move fluidly between the two.

When should we start solids?

Most guidance points to around 6 months[1][2][3], and readiness signs (sitting with support, steady head control, interest in food, loss of the tongue-thrust reflex) matter more than a fixed date. Your pediatrician can confirm the right time for your baby.

What foods should we avoid at first?

Avoid honey before age 1[5], choking hazards (whole grapes, nuts, hard raw vegetables, chunks of meat/cheese), and added salt and sugar. Introduce common allergens one at a time and watch for reactions[1], per your pediatrician's guidance.
