Whole Milk vs. Plant-Based Milk for Toddlers

After age 1, what should go in the cup — and how the options actually compare.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

For most toddlers with no allergy, pediatric guidance points to plain whole cow's milk from 12 to 24 months, then low-fat or skim[1][3] — it delivers protein, the fat young children need for growth[2], and calcium plus vitamin D. Most plant-based milks have less protein and fat and vary a lot in fortification; the closest nutritional match is fortified, unsweetened soy milk[1][2]. Almond, oat, and rice milks are low in protein, so if you use them, protein needs to come from elsewhere. Always choose unsweetened, and check with your pediatrician — especially for allergies or a vegan diet.

Whole cow's milk

The common default after age 1: roughly 8g protein per cup, the fat young children need for growth[2], and naturally rich in calcium (usually with added vitamin D).

Plant-based milk

Ranges widely. Fortified, unsweetened soy is closest to cow's milk in protein; almond, oat, and rice milks are much lower in protein and depend on fortification for calcium and vitamin D.

Which should you choose?

  • No allergy or dietary restrictionWhole cow's milk
  • Dairy allergy or a vegan dietFortified, unsweetened soy milk
  • Lactose intoleranceLactose-free cow's milk or fortified soy
  • Using almond/oat milkOkay as a drink, but get protein from other foods — check with your pediatrician

Frequently asked questions

Is almond milk okay for toddlers?

As an occasional drink it's fine, but almond milk is very low in protein and calories, so it shouldn't replace the nutrition of cow's or fortified soy milk for a toddler. Talk to your pediatrician if it's a main milk.

Which plant milk has the most protein?

Soy milk — it's the only common plant milk with protein close to cow's milk (about 7g per cup), and the AAP considers soy milk an acceptable alternative[1]. Choose a fortified, unsweetened version[2].

When can a toddler start cow's milk?

Whole cow's milk can be introduced at 12 months — not before[2]. Before 1, breast milk or formula remains the primary milk.

How much milk does a toddler need?

The AAP suggests about 2 cups (16 oz) of whole milk a day from 12 to 24 months[1]; a 2019 expert consensus allows 2–3 cups (16–24 oz)[3]. From age 2, switch to low-fat (1%) or skim, up to about 2–2½ cups a day depending on age[3]. Too much milk can crowd out other foods and make it harder to absorb iron[2]. Your pediatrician can tailor this.
