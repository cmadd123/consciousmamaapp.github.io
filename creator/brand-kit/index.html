Typography

MomRise uses Andika throughout. It's warm, rounded, and reads as approachable rather than corporate — that's intentional. Use it whenever you add text to a graphic, not Arial or Times.

Mom's the engine.
She's the one we built it for.
Font: Andika · weights 400 & 700 · free on Google Fonts

If Andika isn't available in your editor, fall back to Inter or your phone's default rounded sans-serif. Never use Times, Comic Sans, or anything italicized at large sizes.

Voice

This is the POV you participate in — the same line that's in the playbook.

"Mom's the engine. We're the toolkit."
She's the one we built it for. Talk to her like she's got it handled — just needs better tools.

If a sentence sounds like it's saving her, rescuing her, or talking down to her, rewrite it. She runs the show; you're handing her a tool.

Do / Don't

The small calls that decide whether a graphic looks "with the brand" or just "made by someone with the colors."

Do
  • Use the warm gradient as a background panel
  • Andika for headlines, your normal iPhone caption for body
  • Code in monospace, big, with letter-spacing
  • Put your code where it has breathing room — not jammed into a corner
  • Show the actual MomRise app on screen (a real screen recording counts)
Don't
  • Recolor the icon or change its corner radius
  • Use a different gradient or substitute palette
  • Set MomRise in italics, all-caps, or stretched type
  • Put the icon inside a circle or another shape
  • Use stock photos of moms with kids that aren't yours
