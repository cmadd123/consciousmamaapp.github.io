# MomRise Traffic Mission — Operating Brief (for the MomRise team + Claude)

*Context and direction for driving organic homeowner— er, parent —traffic to MomRise and converting it to app signups + paid subscriptions. Mirrors the RoofWorks traffic engine, adapted to a parenting app whose hero is a best-in-class meal planner. Written to make Claude reason about our goals, not to be followed line-by-line.*

## The mission in one sentence
Drive an immense volume of parents to MomRise — organically, through **systems, not legwork** — anchored on our **best-in-class meal planner** as the wedge, and convert that traffic to app signups and paid subscriptions.

## The hero: the meal planner
MomRise's main value and sharpest differentiator is the **proprietary, best-in-class meal planner.** Recipe sites have recipes; almost nobody has a genuinely great *kid-and-family-tuned* meal planning system. So meal content is the **traffic wedge** — biggest evergreen search volume, native to Pinterest, and it showcases the hero feature. Activities, milestones, and learning paths are strong secondary clusters, but meal leads.

## The five mental models
1. **Data flywheel, not a static moat.** RoofWorks's moat is storm data it sources externally. Ours is *earned through usage* — the "what real families actually feed their kids and what works" dataset (see data-moats below). It compounds: more use → better data → better planner → more use. A competitor can copy a feature; they cannot copy the behavioral dataset. Build to capture it from day one.
2. **Systems, not legwork.** Hard filter. Tiny team. Anything requiring a human grinding one-at-a-time that can't be templated/automated is OUT. Build-once-serve-forever is IN. Ask of every idea: *"system or legwork?"*
3. **Evergreen, not spiky (the twist in our favor vs. RoofWorks).** Parenting search is massive and *steady* ("toddler meal ideas," "activities for 3 year olds," "milestones at 18 months") — no storm-timing race. Earlier is still better (ranking age), but there's no cliff.
4. **AEO ≠ SEO — and parents ask AI constantly.** Parents ask ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI-Overviews parenting questions all day. Being the *cited* source is enormous, and it's separate work from ranking. AI-referred visitors convert far better.
5. **Content is experiments; signups are the label.** Every page/tool/pin is a test. Measure what gets cited, ranks, and — the metric that matters — **drives app signups.** Double down on winners.

## The data moats to spin up (the strategic core)
MomRise doesn't start with a storm-data-style moat, but it can *manufacture* several — data that accrues from usage and that no recipe site or generic AI has:

1. **Real-family meal-acceptance dataset (the meal-planner moat — build this first).** As families use the planner, capture: what meals kids of each age actually *eat / accept / reject / repeat*, which picky-eater strategies work, seasonal/regional patterns. This is genuinely unique — recipe sites have recipes; we'd have *what real kids actually eat and what works.* Powers (a) best-in-class AI recommendations (the flywheel), (b) data-PR ("State of Toddler Eating 2026," "the 20 meals picky eaters actually accept"), and (c) non-thin programmatic pages ("meals picky 3-year-olds actually eat — from N families").
2. **Structured kid-tuned recipe/nutrition database (available NOW, not usage-gated).** A proprietary, structured meal dataset — every recipe tagged by age-appropriateness, nutrition, prep time, mess level, kid-acceptance, dietary need. This structure IS the "≥3 unique data points per page" that makes programmatic meal pages penalty-proof AND powers the best-in-class planner. Build the structured asset now; it's a moat before any usage data exists.
3. **Real-world milestone data.** Aggregate anonymized tracking → "when kids *actually* hit [milestone]" (vs. textbook ranges). Data-PR + better AI paths + programmatic pages.
4. **Activity-effectiveness data.** Which activities parents actually complete + rate highly by age/mood/energy → "what activities actually work" dataset.
5. **Learning-path outcome data.** Which parenting-challenge solutions parents complete + find helpful → proprietary "what actually helps with [tantrums/sleep/etc.]" data.

**Leading vs. lagging applies to the moat itself:** the *structured* recipe/nutrition database (#2) is available immediately — use it now for programmatic content. The *behavioral* datasets (#1, 3, 4, 5) are LAGGING — they accrue with usage. So instrument the app to capture accept/reject/repeat/rate/complete signals from day one, lead content with the structured data now, and layer the behavioral "what works" data in as it compounds.

## Goal metric hierarchy + leading vs. lagging
Impressions → tool completions (used the free meal generator) → **app signups** → activation (first meal plan / learning path) → **paid subscription / retention (LTV).** The true goal (subscription + retention) is LAGGING and sparse early. So — exactly as with RoofWorks and the reach-out engine — **optimize against the earliest LEADING indicator with volume** (citations, rankings, tool completions, Pinterest saves/clicks, email capture) until subscription/retention history accrues, then **back-propagate expected value** and shift weight to LTV. Guardrail: validate leading indicators still correlate with paid conversion (a page that drives signups who never subscribe is a false win); track each content piece's full downstream path.

## What's IN (systems, ranked)
1. **Programmatic page engine, meal-led** — thousands of data-led pages from the structured recipe DB: "toddler meal ideas by age," "picky-eater meals for [age]," "weekly family meal plan for [dietary need]," "[N]-minute kids dinners," + milestone-by-age + activity-by-age + parenting-challenge guides. ≥3 structured data points each, answer-first, schema, all → app signup. Meal cluster first.
2. **Free meal tool (the "free report" equivalent)** — a genuinely useful free meal-plan / meal-idea generator on the web: a *taste* of the best-in-class planner. The hero link-magnet + funnel to the app. (Plus milestone-checker, activity-finder as secondary tools.)
3. **Pinterest engine (MomRise's standout unconventional lever).** Parenting meal plans + recipes + activities are *Pinterest gold* — evergreen, visual, and *systematizable* (programmatic pinning of meal/activity content at scale). You've already started the Pinterest setup; this is the channel with the best system-to-payoff ratio here.
4. **Self-tuning citation radar + query mining + gap detection + winner amplification** — same loop as RoofWorks, optimizing for app signups.
5. **Data-PR** — "State of Family Eating" reports from the behavioral dataset (once it accrues) → press + backlinks + AI citations.
6. **Creator revshare (already in flight)** — your ~200K-follower creators are the warm/influencer distribution complement to the organic engine. Systematize the creator content kit.
7. **Comparison-authority engine (source-data aggregation — the WiFiHotshots play; likely the FASTEST authority path).** How WiFiHotshots outranked Cisco/HPE: they aggregated the OEMs' OWN authoritative spec data into neutral side-by-side comparisons and owned the "X vs Y" / "best X" queries incumbents can't own. Replicate it — and note it **needs NO recipe library**, so it sidesteps the sourcing gate entirely. **Mechanism:** aggregate authoritative FACTUAL source data (specs, nutrition panels, ingredient lists, guidelines) and structure it into comprehensive neutral comparisons. Low lift (aggregate, don't create), authoritative (from source), legal (facts aren't copyrightable), and exactly what LLMs cite. Moat = the aggregation + structure + neutrality, not proprietary data.
   - **The "OEM-data equivalent" for parenting:** manufacturer specs (baby/kid gear — dimensions, weight limits, safety certs), nutrition panels + ingredient lists (formula/food/snack brands — from labels/USDA), authoritative method sources (AAP/pediatric guidelines, research).
   - **Comparisons to own (ADJACENT to the app, NON-participant categories — per Brennan):** "Formula A vs B" (nutrition panels), "best high chairs for baby-led weaning" (specs), "baby-led weaning vs purées" (guidelines/research), "Montessori vs traditional activities." **Method comparisons especially** — least covered by product-review sites, most LLM-queried, funnel straight into meal planning + learning paths.
   - **Guardrails:** don't neutrally compare competitor *apps* (you're a participant) — stick to non-participant products/methods; only aggregate the *facts* — never copy protected expression (marketing prose, review text, photos).

## What's OUT (per the systems-not-legwork filter)
- Manual community grinding (Reddit/Facebook-group/LinkedIn one-post-at-a-time) — legwork. (Reddit is AI-cited but can't be automated; skip as an initiative, answer organically only.)
- Manual influencer DMs beyond the systematized creator program.
- Referral/partner BD until bigger (embeddable tools earn passive backlinks — that's fine).
- Manual video — unless *programmatic* (AI-generated per-topic meal/activity videos with transcripts), run as a measured pilot only.

## Free tools to build (each = ranking page + link magnet + funnel to app)
1. **Free meal-plan / meal-idea generator** (the hero — taste of the planner)
2. **Picky-eater meal finder by age**
3. **Milestone checker by age**
4. **Activity finder** (by mood/age/energy/mess)
5. **Learning-path preview** (a parenting challenge → sample steps)

## Positioning / guardrail
Unlike RoofWorks, we don't start with an external data moat — so **content quality is the moat.** Google penalizes thin AI content hard; our protection is the *structured* recipe/nutrition data + real family behavioral data making every page genuinely useful and specific. Best-in-class or don't ship it.

## Sequenced start
1. **Now:** build the structured recipe/nutrition DB (the moat + the non-thin data layer) + the free meal generator + the meal-led programmatic page engine; get indexed. Instrument accept/reject/repeat capture from day one.
2. **Parallel:** stand up the Pinterest engine (programmatic meal/activity pins) + AEO hygiene (schema, answer-first, allow AI crawlers, Bing/IndexNow).
3. **As data accrues:** behavioral-data-powered content + "State of Family Eating" data-PR + self-tuning loop tied to signups→subscriptions.
